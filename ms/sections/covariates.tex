\section{Regression coefficients and their uncertainty}\label{sec:covariates}
\subsection{An aggregate used as the outcome}
With a fully observed, full-rank design $X$, let
$A=(X^{\mathsf T}X)^{-1}X^{\mathsf T}$. Regressing an aggregate outcome
$Cy$ on $X$ gives $\beta_y=ACy$; using $Cq$ gives $\beta_q=ACq$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outcomeregression}
\beta_q-\beta_y=ACe,\qquad
\E[\beta_q-\beta_y]=ACb,\qquad
\Cov(\beta_q-\beta_y)=AC\Sigma C^{\mathsf T}A^{\mathsf T},
\end{equation}
where $b=\E[e]$ and $\Sigma=\Cov(e)$ refer to a stated prediction-error
process conditional on the fixed panel. A coefficient contrast has item
weights $w=C^{\mathsf T}A^{\mathsf T}r$. Equations~\eqref{eq:contrast} and
\eqref{eq:outcomeregression} therefore cover outcome-regression coefficients
as well as means and group comparisons.

Even independent errors on distinct items need not give independent errors
across records. If $\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(v_j)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainreusecov}
\Cov(d_i,d_\ell)=\sum_jc_{ij}c_{\ell j}v_j.
\end{equation}
The same item shock appears wherever its loading is nonzero. A record-level
variance calculation that discards these covariances can be too small for
positive averages or too large for signed contrasts. The exact direction
depends on the coefficient's item weights, not just item popularity.
This is the common-source structure studied in shift-share inference;
its identification and asymptotic assumptions still need to hold in the
particular application \citep{adao2019,borusyak2022shiftshare}.

Equation~\eqref{eq:outcomeregression} quantifies the label-error component.
For a population regression that also treats the complete-label outcome as
random, write $T=X\beta+u$. With $\Omega=\Cov(u)$ and
$K=\Cov(u,e)$, conditional on $X,C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:combinedvariance}
\Var(\beta_q)=A\{\Omega+C\Sigma C^{\mathsf T}
                 +KC^{\mathsf T}+CK^{\mathsf T}\}A^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
The cross terms vanish under additional restrictions such as independence
of $u$ and $e$. Estimating only $C\Sigma C^{\mathsf T}$ does not estimate
this whole variance. Corrections for predicted outcomes must specify the
relationship between the observed outcome, its prediction, and the downstream
regressors \citep{wang2020postprediction}.

\subsection{An aggregate used as a covariate}
Let $Y$ be an observed outcome and let $V$ be fully observed controls,
including an intercept. Put $M_V=I-V(V^{\mathsf T}V)^{-1}V^{\mathsf T}$.
The complete-label coefficient on $T=Cy$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truecovariate}
\beta(y)=\frac{y^{\mathsf T}C^{\mathsf T}M_VY}
                    {y^{\mathsf T}C^{\mathsf T}M_VCy}
        =\frac{N(y)}{D(y)},\qquad D(y)>0.
\end{equation}
For $t=M_VCy$, $h=M_VCe$, and $u=M_VY-\beta(y)t$, the exact change is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covariateerror}
\beta(q)-\beta(y)=
\frac{h^{\mathsf T}u-\beta(y)
          \{t^{\mathsf T}h+h^{\mathsf T}h\}}
     {(t+h)^{\mathsf T}(t+h)},
\end{equation}
provided the predicted covariate has positive residual variation.
The denominator changes as well as the numerator, and the linear and
quadratic error terms need not have the same sign. Ordinary attenuation is
therefore not a general description.

For small perturbations, the coefficient's item gradient is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regressiongradient}
\lambda=\frac{C^{\mathsf T}M_VY-2\beta(y)C^{\mathsf T}M_VCy}{D(y)}.
\end{equation}
Thus $\lambda^{\mathsf T}b$ and $\lambda^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\lambda$ are
first-order bias and variance expressions only when the remainder in
equation~\eqref{eq:covariateerror} is negligible on the relevant scale.
They are not feasible SEs unless the unknown labels and error moments can
be replaced by sufficiently accurate estimates. Weak residual variation in
$Cy$, or a few influential items, can make the linear approximation poor.

These are generated-covariate problems in the sense of
\citet{fong2021predictions}, \citet{battaglia2025generated}, and
\citet{christensen2026guide}. Reuse makes their input construction and
validation unit explicit. A verified item changes pieces of several rows;
it usually does not reveal any row's complete covariate. Results based on
validated complete rows require an adaptation or a different validation
design \citep{kluger2025imputed}. An item with the same loading in every
row has $M_VC_{\cdot j}=0$, so its error cancels from this coefficient even
if it dominates unadjusted totals.

\subsection{Several constructed variables}
If both regression sides use the same predictions, the numerator in
equation~\eqref{eq:truecovariate} becomes
$y^{\mathsf T}C_1^{\mathsf T}M_VC_0y$. Shared errors now affect both
regressor--outcome and regressor--regressor cross-products. For constructed
covariates $C_ry$, the repeated-prediction error cross-product is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maincrossnormal}
\E[d_r^{\mathsf T}M_Vd_s]
=b^{\mathsf T}C_r^{\mathsf T}M_VC_sb
 +\operatorname{tr}(C_r^{\mathsf T}M_VC_s\Sigma).
\end{equation}
It can be nonzero with independent item errors. Correcting the marginal
error of each constructed variable does not determine these joint terms.
Correlated outcome and exposure errors are already studied in measurement
error models \citep{shaw2021correlated}; here their relationship follows
from the shared item labels and the two loading matrices.

\subsection{Conditions for standard-error interpretations}
Bias, variability, and coverage must be assessed separately. Some
generated-covariate asymptotic regimes have consistent conventional robust
SEs but a biased limiting distribution around the desired coefficient
\citep{christensen2026guide,battaglia2025generated}. Reuse does not rule out
such a regime. It requires checking its laws of large numbers, central limit
conditions, and variance-estimation assumptions for the actual loadings.

For a linear estimate with independent item errors, put
$s^2=\sum_jw_j^2v_j$. The sufficient Lindeberg condition
$\max_j|w_j|/s\to0$ gives a normal limit for centered label error. It need
not hold as the number of records grows: those records may continue to load
on the same few uncertain items. Even a small maximum variance share is
insufficient when errors become rare (Appendix~\ref{app:prediction}).
Clustering by record does not recover cross-record item covariance, while
assigning each record one item cluster omits its other memberships.
Resampling must preserve the sources that the inferential model treats as
random \citep{owen2012arrays}. Shared classifier fitting can additionally
correlate errors on distinct items; that is a separate mechanism from
reusing one item's error.
