\section{Aggregates in regression}
\label{sec:covariates}
An aggregate built from predicted labels can be either a regression outcome
or a covariate. If it is the outcome and $X$ is a fully observed, full-rank
design, the complete-label least-squares coefficients are
$\beta^*=ACy$, where $A=(X^{\mathsf T}X)^{-1}X^{\mathsf T}$. The
prediction-based coefficients are $ACq$. A contrast $r^{\mathsf T}\beta^*$
has item weights $w=C^{\mathsf T}A^{\mathsf T}r$, so its error is $w^{\mathsf T}e$.
The count bounds and item-audit correction below apply to this contrast.
The coefficients are descriptive for the observed frame; causal or population
interpretations require additional assumptions.

If the aggregate is a covariate, the coefficient is nonlinear in the labels.
The same is true of a proportion whose denominator depends on predicted
labels. The linear bounds below do not apply to these quantities without
evaluating their full downstream map.

For example, let $Y$ be an observed outcome and $V$ a full-rank matrix of
fully observed controls, including an intercept. The coefficient that
ordinary least squares would report with complete item labels is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truecovariate}
 \beta(y)=\frac{T^{\mathsf T}M_VY}{T^{\mathsf T}M_VT}
 =\frac{y^{\mathsf T}C^{\mathsf T}M_VY}
        {y^{\mathsf T}C^{\mathsf T}M_VCy},
 \qquad M_V=I-V(V^{\mathsf T}V)^{-1}V^{\mathsf T},
\end{equation}
when the denominator is positive. Both numerator and denominator change
when labels change, so ordinary attenuation is not a general conclusion.
The exact error identity and item-audit moment formulas are in
Appendix~\ref{app:covariates}. Those formulas do not provide an interval for
$\beta(y)$: a ratio of estimated moments can be biased, unstable, or
undefined.

\citet{fong2021predictions} study regression with predicted covariates, and
\citet{battaglia2025generated} include indices built by classification and
aggregation. This paper's item-to-record map makes the unit of validation
explicit: verifying item $j$ may correct pieces of many regression rows,
without revealing any row's complete covariate. Work using validated complete
regression rows, including \citet{kluger2025imputed}, requires a different
sampling unit or an adaptation here. Overlap in $C$ has the common-shock
structure of shift-share designs \citep{adao2019}. It can matter in either
direction for a coefficient. An item
with the same weight in every unit affects their totals, but its error disappears
from a regression with an intercept because $M_VC_{\cdot j}=0$.

There is also a generated-regressor issue beyond the covariance of the rows.
The OLS numerator is linear in the labels but its denominator contains
$G=C^{\mathsf T}M_VC$, including cross-products of items that enter the
same units after residualizing on controls. Appendix~\ref{app:covariates} gives the exact
plug-in score and its expectation under a specified prediction-error process.
If the same predictions build two or more covariates, their measurement errors
also enter the off-diagonal cross-products of the regression's normal
equations. In that case there need not be a single attenuation factor: an
error shared by two covariates can move both coefficients. The relevant
cross-products are given in Appendix~\ref{app:covariates}.
A verified item can inform many units' aggregates. For
equation~\eqref{eq:truecovariate}, an item audit must account for item pairs
in the quadratic denominator; correcting each aggregate separately is not
enough.

A model-based target requires different assumptions. If information
$\mathcal H$ contains $C,V$ and the prediction inputs, and
$p_j=\E[y_j\mid\mathcal H]$, then $Cp=\E[T\mid\mathcal H]$.
Under $Y=V\gamma+\beta T+\varepsilon$ with
$\E[\varepsilon\mid\mathcal H]=0$ and a full-rank design, regressing $Y$
on $(V,Cp)$ identifies the population $\beta$. Marginal score calibration
across items does not establish these conditional means or the exclusion
assumption. This population coefficient is also distinct from the fixed-sample
complete-label coefficient in equation~\eqref{eq:truecovariate}.
