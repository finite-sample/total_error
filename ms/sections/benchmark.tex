\section{Applications}\label{sec:applications}
\subsection{Reuse in a June 2022 browsing sample}\label{sec:benchmark}
The YouGov Pulse/RealityMine browsing sample contains
\BenchmarkUsers{} users, \BenchmarkDomains{} domains,
\BenchmarkRecords{} user--domain records, and \BenchmarkVisits{} visits.
Domain labels recur across users, and visits are concentrated: the most
widely used domain appears in \LargestDomainUsers{} users' records. One
domain accounts for \LargestDomainVisitShare{}\% of visits, while the most
visited 1\% of domains account for \TopOnePercentDomainVisitShare{}\%.
These are properties of this observed sample, before a prediction-error
model is introduced.

We use seven recorded Blacklight scanner outcomes as reference labels for
an illustration. Two count outcomes are dichotomized at a positive value;
the other five are binary. Shares use visits to domains with recorded
scanner labels as their denominator. The scanner labels measure recorded
tracking behavior rather than every form of tracking. Regressions include
an intercept and indicators for gender, race, education, and age groups;
the omitted categories are Male, White, HS or Below, and under 25.
Their twelve non-intercept coefficients are descriptive for this panel.

To illustrate information loss from counts, we impose errors by randomly
flipping the integer part of 1\%, 5\%, or 10\% of the domain labels within
each reference class. At the 1\% setting, exact false-positive and
false-negative counts leave the signs of all \BenchmarkContrasts{}
visit-share coefficients unidentified. The bound is computed separately
for each coefficient, so the extreme allocations need not hold jointly.
For the female coefficient on Facebook-pixel visit share, the reference
value is \FocalTruth{} percentage points and the random predictions differ
by \FocalRealizedError{} percentage points. Yet their count-compatible
range for the reference coefficient is
$[\FocalLower{},\FocalUpper{}]$ percentage points.
Appendix~\ref{app:browsing} reports the outcome-specific bounds.
This calculation describes what the imposed error counts fail to exclude;
it does not estimate a trained classifier's likely error.

The same observed visit matrix illustrates a constructed covariate.
For each other scanner outcome, we regress user-level visit share on
advertising-tracker visit share and the demographic controls. We hold the
outcome labels fixed and replace only the advertising-tracker labels with
the 1\% random-error draw. The third-party-cookie slope changes from
\CompleteCovariateCoefficient{} to \ImposedCovariateCoefficient{};
the six slopes can rise or fall (Table~\ref{tab:covariate}).
These are coefficient changes under one imposed draw. The coverage
experiment in Section~\ref{sec:simulation} addresses the separate SE question.

The matrix also shows how reuse affects the label-error covariance under
a stipulated model. Suppose distinct domain errors are independent with
equal variance. Equation~\eqref{eq:overlapratio} compares a statistic's
label-error variance with a calculation that keeps each user's marginal
variance but discards covariance between users. The ratio for mean visit
share is \MeanShareVarianceRatio{}; one domain contributes
\MeanShareLargestItem{}\% of the item-level variance. The female and Black
coefficient ratios are \FemaleShareVarianceRatio{} and
\BlackShareVarianceRatio{}, respectively. The latter illustrates
cancellation in a signed contrast.

Under this same model, errors in the female and male mean visit shares
have correlation \GroupErrorCorrelation{}. Their difference has only
\GroupDifferenceVarianceFraction{}\% of the error variance obtained by
treating the two group-mean errors as independent. These calculations use
the observed loadings and an assumed error covariance. They diagnose the
consequence of dropping reuse; they do not estimate an actual classifier's
SE. A regression correction for actual predictions would additionally
require the validation information described in Section~\ref{sec:inference}.
