\section{Reuse and error locations in browsing data}\label{sec:benchmark}
Domain labels are reused across people, and visits are concentrated on a few
domains. The June 2022 YouGov Pulse/RealityMine data contain
\BenchmarkUsers{} pseudonymized users, \BenchmarkDomains{} domains,
\BenchmarkRecords{} user--domain records, and \BenchmarkVisits{} visits.
The most widely used domain appears in \LargestDomainUsers{} users' records.
One domain accounts for \LargestDomainVisitShare{}\% of visits; the most visited
1\% of domains account for \TopOnePercentDomainVisitShare{}\%.
We use Blacklight scanner outcomes and include domains with recorded
scanner labels. Each user's visit shares use visits to those domains as the
denominator.
The scanner outcomes serve as reference labels for this exercise,
not error-free measures of all tracking activity. The exercise makes no causal
or population-representative claim about demographic differences.

We use an intercept and indicators for gender, race, education, and age groups.
Omitted categories are Male, White,
HS or Below, and under 25. For each of seven scanner outcomes, we
calculate all twelve non-intercept least-squares coefficients for both visit
totals and visit shares. Each coefficient is a fixed weighted label sum.
Two scanner columns are counts and are dichotomized at a positive value; the
other five are binary.

Predictions are imposed, rather than produced by a fitted classifier. Within
each true class, we randomly flip the integer part of 1\%, 5\%, or 10\% of the
labels. We then perform two distinct exercises. First, holding those predictions
fixed, Proposition~\ref{prop:bounds} gives the true-coefficient range compatible
with their exact confusion counts. Second, holding the reference labels fixed,
we rearrange the same numbers of false positives and false negatives to minimize
or maximize the predicted coefficient. The latter asks whether two classifiers
with identical confusion matrices can report opposing comparisons. It is a
worst-case sensitivity analysis, not an estimate of likely classifier bias.
Extrema are computed separately for each coefficient; a single error arrangement
need not reverse all comparisons at once.

At the 1\% setting, exact confusion counts do not identify the sign of any
of the \BenchmarkContrasts{} visit-share coefficients (Table~\ref{tab:browsing}).

The realized errors from random placement are
usually much smaller than the count-compatible ranges. For example, the
reference female coefficient for Facebook-pixel visit share is
\FocalTruth{} percentage points. The fixed random predictions differ by
\FocalRealizedError{} percentage points, while their count-compatible
interval for the reference coefficient is
$[\FocalLower{},\FocalUpper{}]$ percentage points. Moving the same numbers
of errors can reverse all \BenchmarkContrasts{} share-coefficient signs,
but those placements are adversarial. These results show what the confusion
counts fail to rule out for this frame, not what a fitted classifier is likely
to do.

The same browsing matrix gives a regression-covariate illustration. For each
other scanner outcome, we regress its user-level visit share on the
advertising-tracker visit share and the demographic indicators above. We hold
the outcome labels fixed and replace only the advertising-tracker labels with

\paragraph{How much does domain reuse matter for uncertainty?}
The incidence matrix permits a separate diagnostic without fitting a
classifier. Suppose each domain's prediction error is independent of the
others and has the same variance, as under independent binary flips with an
equal false-positive and false-negative probability. Equation~\eqref{eq:overlapratio}
compares the prediction-error variance of a linear user statistic with a
calculation that keeps each user's marginal error variance but discards
covariance between users. For the mean visit share the variance ratio is
\MeanShareVarianceRatio{}, and one domain accounts for
\MeanShareLargestItem{}\% of the item-level variance under this model.
The female and Black share-coefficient ratios are
\FemaleShareVarianceRatio{} and \BlackShareVarianceRatio{}: shared domain
errors can cancel in signed contrasts. These ratios describe the observed
weights under stipulated errors, not uncertainty for a fitted classifier.
More directly, the same predicted domain labels enter the female and male
mean visit shares. Their prediction errors have correlation
\GroupErrorCorrelation{} under the equal-variance item model. The error
variance of the female-minus-male difference is only
\GroupDifferenceVarianceFraction{}\% of a calculation that treats the two
group-mean errors as independent. Skew in the domain weights produces strong
common movement in the two means, while differencing removes much of it.

\paragraph{An audit can retain more.}
Appendix~\ref{app:audit} evaluates count projection and direct weighted
residual estimation on the same probability-audit draws for one declared
coefficient. The count certificate covered that coefficient in every draw
but never settled its sign. The direct normal intervals were narrower but
undercovered in the smallest random design; the finite-sample Hoeffding
baseline was wider than count projection. The exercise illustrates the
information lost by counts and the limits of these particular intervals. It
does not compare efficient finite-sample procedures or assess the theorem's
simultaneous utility across the other coefficients.
