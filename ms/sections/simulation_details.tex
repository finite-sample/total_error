\section{Simulation construction and additional results}\label{app:simulation}
For each panel, the observed characteristic $x_i$ is equally spaced on
$[-1,1]$, controls are an intercept and $\sin(i)$, and item features $z_j$
are equally spaced on $[-1.5,1.5]$. Labels are $y_j=1\{z_j>0\}$.
Each record receives 80 multinomial visits with probabilities proportional
to $p_j\exp(x_i z_j)$. Baseline popularity is $p_j=1$. In the concentrated
design, $p_1=3(k-1)$, $z_1=1.5$, and $y_1=1$. The resulting visit shares
form $C_1$. Duration shares $C_0$ reweight its columns by $\exp\{\cos(j)\}$
and renormalize each row. The observed outcome in the covariate case is
$Y_i=0.8(C_1y)_i+0.1x_i+0.02\cos(i)$. These numerical constants define
the simulation rather than estimate a behavioral model.

The outcome regression uses $C_1q$ as outcome and $x$ as covariate. The
covariate regression uses the observed $Y$ as outcome and $C_1q$ as
covariate. The both-sides regression uses $C_0q$ as outcome and $C_1q$ as
covariate. All include the stated controls. Independent item prediction
flips have probability 0.15, while independent audit inclusion has
probability 0.70. Their common item indicators are reused wherever a label
appears. Audit corrections condition on each realized prediction vector;
their moment-unbiasedness therefore also holds when averaging over these
prediction draws.

\section{Computing the joint moment covariance}\label{app:momentvariance}
For independent Bernoulli verification, let $L_j$ be the vector of
singleton contributions in equation~\eqref{eq:polynomialaudit}, and let
$H_{j\ell}=H_{\ell j}$ be its pair-contribution vector, with $H_{jj}=0$.
Put $\xi_j=R_j/\pi_j-1$ and $t_j=(1-\pi_j)/\pi_j$. Expanding the audit
error gives the orthogonal decomposition
\[
\widehat{\boldsymbol m}-\boldsymbol m
=\sum_j\xi_j\nu_j+\sum_{j<\ell}\xi_j\xi_\ell H_{j\ell},
\qquad \nu_j=L_j+\sum_{\ell\ne j}H_{j\ell}.
\]
Independence and centering make distinct terms uncorrelated, so the oracle
conditional covariance is
\[
V=\sum_jt_j\nu_j\nu_j^{\mathsf T}
 +\sum_{j<\ell}t_jt_\ell H_{j\ell}H_{j\ell}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
For a feasible calculation, define
$\widehat L_j=R_jL_j/\pi_j$,
$\widehat H_{j\ell}=R_jR_\ell H_{j\ell}/(\pi_j\pi_\ell)$, and
$s_j=\widehat L_j+\sum_{\ell\ne j}\widehat H_{j\ell}$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:polynomialvariance} reduces to
\[
\widehat V=\sum_j(1-\pi_j)s_js_j^{\mathsf T}
 -\sum_{j<\ell}(1-\pi_j)(1-\pi_\ell)
          \widehat H_{j\ell}\widehat H_{j\ell}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The first term counts a pair's diagonal covariance twice, once through each
member. The subtraction supplies the required correction. The expression
is unbiased but need not be positive semidefinite in every audit.

This decomposition also makes a normal approximation's requirements
concrete. For a coefficient gradient $g$, the linear audit projection has
variance $s^2=\sum_jt_j(g^{\mathsf T}\nu_j)^2$. A sufficient Lindeberg
condition is
$\max_j|g^{\mathsf T}\nu_j|/(\pi_js)\to0$. The pair remainder must have
variance $\sum_{j<\ell}t_jt_\ell(g^{\mathsf T}H_{j\ell})^2=o(s^2)$.
Variance-estimator consistency and a negligible Taylor remainder for the
regression map are additional requirements. These conditions concern a
sequence of panels and audits; they are not established merely by having
many downstream records.
